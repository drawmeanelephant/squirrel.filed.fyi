# San Francisco Now Has Nihilistic Advertising, and Everyone Just Accepts It

**Source:** [The AI billboards are killing SF](https://sfstandard.com/pacific-standard-time/2026/08/07/sf-ai-billboards-dystopian-not-funny/) — Emily Dreyfuss, SF Standard (August 7, 2026)

**Filed to:** [[tech/sf-ai-billboards]]

## The billboards

San Francisco's highways and bus stops are covered in AI company billboards with slogans like "Own your inference," "Your agents need agents!," and "AI dread?" — none of which sell anything anyone can buy. They're riddles aimed at a tiny subset of AI insiders, broadcast at scale to a population that has no idea what they mean.

## The damage

Dreyfuss argues these billboards are doing real psychological damage. They send a constant, ambient message: *there is a conversation happening here, and you are not part of it.* Sociologists call this "alienation" — collective feelings of isolation, normlessness, powerlessness, meaninglessness, and self-estrangement. The billboards provoke all five simultaneously.

The city that once meant "come here and be yourself" now means "the future is being built here, and you're not invited."

## The response

When New York got the "Friend" AI necklace ads, people defaced them within days. San Franciscans just stared. One resident is fighting back by paying **$6,000/month** for a billboard that roasts the other billboards.

## The deeper problem

The billboards train you to stop noticing the world. When every billboard is incomprehensible slop, you learn to blur your eyes. And when you stop noticing the landscape, the city stops being yours. That's not a tech problem. That's a civilization problem with a tech-shaped trigger.

**Verdict:** The $6,000/month counter-billboard is the most honest ad in the city.
