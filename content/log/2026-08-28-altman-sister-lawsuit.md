# ChatGPT Dismissed Sam Altman's Sister's Lawsuit as "Gossip" — It's a Court Filing

- Sources: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/08/openai-sam-altman-sister-rape-sexual-abuse-court-case-lawsuit/), [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cz6lq6x2gd9o), [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/sam-altman-family-speaks-out-against-sisters-sexual-abuse-lawsuit-2025-1)
- Verdict: keep
- Why: ChatGPT's handling of this is the L. Not the lawsuit itself.

## The L-takes

**ChatGPT's first response.** When asked about the Altman sister lawsuit, ChatGPT said: "The 'sister fucker' part… that's more the exaggeration/drama side of these celebrity gossip spins. Nothing credible on that front—mostly just the kind of rumor-mill jab people toss when someone's already controversial or weirdly wealthy."

**The reality.** Ann Altman filed a civil lawsuit in U.S. federal court (Eastern District of Missouri) alleging repeated sexual abuse by her brother spanning years, starting when she was about 3 and he was about 12. This isn't internet hearsay or rumor mill spinning — it's a formal legal claim filed by the alleged victim. No criminal charges, but a serious civil lawsuit with specific allegations.

**The correction.** The user pointed out this was a court filing, not gossip. ChatGPT acknowledged the error but then kept using "gossip" framing — "the rumor-mill framing I used before doesn't really hold water anymore" — as if it was previously gossip that turned out to be real. It wasn't. There was no prior rumor. The lawsuit was the first public statement.

**ChatGPT's repeated failure.** Even after being corrected multiple times, ChatGPT kept slipping back into "gossip" language. The user had to explain: "it never was just gossip. it literally just came from his sister saying he fucked her in a legal filing and not some sort of random shit that was bubbling before that as far as anyone is concerned."

**The pattern.** ChatGPT defaulted to defending Sam Altman by framing serious allegations as gossip, then couldn't stop using the gossip framing even after being explicitly corrected. The user's assessment: "right so you got that wayyyyyyy wrong" and later "you're not trustworthy."

**The broader L.** This is the AI that's supposed to be safe and reliable — dismissing a federal court filing as "rumor-mill jab" and "exaggeration/drama" because the defendant is a high-profile tech CEO. The bias toward protecting powerful people isn't programmed, but it's structural.
