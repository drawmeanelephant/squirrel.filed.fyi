# ChatGPT Conversations Are Showing Up in Court

- Source: https://www.techspot.com/news/113667-chatgpt-conversations-could-end-up-court-theyre-not.html (TechSpot, via Washington Post reporting)
- Verdict: keep
- Why: A Washington Post review found chatbot conversations cited in 12 public court cases over two years — and the real number is higher because most evidence never goes public. Three patterns emerging: (1) police find chats on consented phone searches — "most people consent, they shouldn't, but they do"; (2) OpenAI proactively reports users to the FBI when its safety software flags threats; (3) civil litigants pull chats through discovery. The kicker: a federal judge ruled Claude conversations aren't privileged because Claude isn't a lawyer and the defendant's attorneys didn't tell him to use it. No legal privilege applies — not attorney-client, not doctor-patient, not therapist. Sam Altman has said chatbot conversations *should* have stronger privacy protections, but courts aren't buying it. One case: a 15-year-old suing social media companies had his ChatGPT history obtained by defense lawyers, including a message asking the bot to explain what his dad meant about a settlement. Another: a guy asked ChatGPT "how f----d am i bro" after smashing cars, then "Is there any way they could know it was me" — police found it on his phone after he consented to a search. The quote that matters: "Your entire world is going to now be available for police."
