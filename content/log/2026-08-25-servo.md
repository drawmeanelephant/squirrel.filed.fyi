# Servo

- Source: [servo.org](https://servo.org/) (sent by me, 2026-08-25)
- Verdict: keep — promote-to-tech, and it belongs in the same breath as
  [[mac/omniweb]]

## The arc

Servo was Mozilla's experimental browser engine — the project Rust was
built to prove, the research engine whose WebRender and Stylo components
ended up powering Firefox. Then August 2020: Mozilla's layof-the-year
guts the team, the engine goes on the shelf, everyone writes the
eulogy. 2023: the project lands at Linux Foundation Europe with
[Igalia](https://www.igalia.com/) driving development. And in 2026 the
thing ships *monthly*: "March in Servo" was its biggest month ever,
FreeBSD support landed, there's a new Android UI, DevTools with
blackboxing, SharedWorker, experimental Sanitizer API, published
[Web Platform Test pass rates](https://servo.org/wpt/), and an embeddable
WebView API targeting desktop, mobile, OpenHarmony, and embedded
devices.

## Why it matters here

The [[mac/omniweb]] elegy ends with browsers as "a war between three
engine owners who all sell advertising." Servo is the counter-evidence
that the argument can win institutionally: a fourth engine with no ad
revenue to serve, memory-safe by construction (Rust), open governance,
funded as public-interest infrastructure — Sovereign Tech Agency (that's
the German government), NLnet, Futurewei, individual patrons via Open
Collective. An engine surviving because governments and foundations
decided a monoculture is a hazard, not because it monetizes attention.

The OmniWeb verdict was "lost the market, won the argument." Servo is
what it looks like when the argument gets a budget line. Mozilla shelved
it; the shelf turned out to be an ark.

## The fine print

It's an embedding engine first — you don't switch to Servo for daily
browsing yet. WPT pass rates are published honestly, which is the
opposite of vaporware behavior. Watch it, embed it, and if a certain
Zig-based site compiler ever wants a web view, this is the one that
wouldn't rat you out to an ad exchange.
