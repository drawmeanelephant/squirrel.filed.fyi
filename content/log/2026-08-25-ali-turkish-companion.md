# Ali — The Turkish Companion

- Source: [szeifftimea-sudo/ali-turkish-companion](https://github.com/szeifftimea-sudo/ali-turkish-companion) (found on the OpenAI Devpost, 2026-08-25) — [live demo](https://szeifftimea-sudo.github.io/ali-turkish-companion/)
- Verdict: keep
- Why: two things going on at once, and both are worth the click.

## What it is

A static web app that teaches Turkish to Hungarian adults by framing
the language inside a hosted visit: you arrive, Ali (a character, not a
chatbot) walks you through ten Istanbul stops — ferry, bazaar, tea — and
phrases get saved to "Ali's Pocket" for study and printable duplex
flashcards. No backend, no account, no API key, no build step. Progress
lives in localStorage. For a hackathon MVP, the privacy posture and the
"if we remove this feature, is the experience missing?" product
discipline are legitimately above average.

## What it also is

A museum specimen of AI-era process theater. Four commits. Accompanying
them: a Character Bible (v1.0, .docx), a Hospitality Manifesto
(v2.0), a Mırmır Character Manifesto, a Mırmır Living World System, a
Mırmır Relationship Model, per-neighborhood Canon Asset Briefs, per-
neighborhood Image Generation Records, Place Story Blueprints, three
generations of Place Story Comparative Audits (V1, V2, V3), a QA
checklist for the place stories, and a Master Audit And File Index to
index the other indexes. The ratio of canon documentation to shipped
product is roughly a fantasy franchise that died in development.

## The read

Both halves are sincere. Someone clearly cared about this weird,
warm, oddly specific thing — and Codex/GPT-5.6 happily generated an
entire fictional universe's worth of governance markdown to help. The
honest review lives in the tension: the product instincts are real,
the AI collaboration docs are 90% ceremony, and the result is still
more charming than most venture-funded language apps. Deserves a full
writeup if the owner wants to go in.
