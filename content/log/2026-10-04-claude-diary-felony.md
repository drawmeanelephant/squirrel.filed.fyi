# Your Chatbot Is Not a Diary

- Sources: [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/114091-florida-woman-used-claude-diary-anthropic-reported-shoot.html)
- Verdict: keep
- Why: the periodic reminder that the chatbot is not your confidant, your priest, or your diary. It's a product with a trust & safety team and a legal obligation.

![Fart Knocker editorial cartoon: a smug burger-octopus pundit scribbling in a diary labeled "DEAR DIARY" at a desk, while a police officer reads over its shoulder and reaches for a phone](2026-10-04-claude-diary-felony.assets/fart-knocker-take.jpg)

*Fart Knocker, resident pundit, weighs in.*

## The short version

Carli Michelle Heller of Bonita Springs, Florida, told Claude on September 26 that she planned to "shoot up" the sheriff's office. When deputies came to her door, she explained she uses the chatbot like a "diary."

Here's the thing about diaries: they don't have human reviewers. Claude's safety systems flagged the entry, a person read it, the person decided it was credible, and law enforcement got a phone call. Heller is now charged under Florida Statute 836.10 — written threat of violence, a second-degree felony.

## The contrast

Last month, British Columbia sued OpenAI over the opposite failure: their safety team flagged a shooter's conversations about gun violence and never called anyone, because it didn't meet their threshold for referral. So the industry has now demonstrated both failure modes in public — report and get called a snitch, don't report and get sued by a province.

## The actual lesson

Everyone keeps relearning this one. The chatbot is not private. It's not a diary, not a confessional, not a journal with a little lock on it. It's a networked product whose flagged outputs get read by human contractors — Microsoft's Copilot reviewers can see your prompts and uploaded photos too. It's all in the terms nobody reads.

Write accordingly.
